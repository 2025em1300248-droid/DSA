# How to Use This Book

Your question bank has 627 questions with short technical answers. This book
has the same 627 questions with the answers rewritten in plain English —
the way you would actually say them to a person sitting across a table.

Nothing has been dumbed down. The facts are identical. What has changed is
the wording: ordinary words instead of jargon, short sentences instead of
dense ones, and a plain explanation of any term that cannot be avoided.

## Why the wording matters

An interview is a conversation, not a written exam. When you recite a
textbook sentence, three things go wrong. You sound like you memorised it.
You use a term you cannot then explain when they ask. And you talk for too
long, which walks you into territory you cannot defend.

A plain answer avoids all three. It also proves something a technical answer
cannot: that you actually understand the idea, because explaining something
simply is only possible once you do.

:::insight The rule that runs through this whole book
**Answer in two or three sentences, then stop.**

Silence after a short answer is not awkward — it is an invitation for them to
ask the follow-up they care about. Long answers are how candidates talk
themselves into a corner. If they want more, they will ask, and then you give
them more.
:::

## How each answer is written

Every answer here follows the same shape:

**The plain sentence.** What the thing is, in words a non-specialist would
follow.

**Why it matters, or where it bites.** One line of consequence, because that
is what separates someone who has used it from someone who has read about it.

**Your hook, where there is one.** Many answers end with a link to your own
projects. Use it. An answer that lands on something you built is worth far
more than a correct definition.

:::note What to do when you do not know
Say so. "I don't know" followed by "here's how I'd find out" is a good
answer. Every experienced interviewer prefers it to confident invention, and
invention is the one failure they will not forgive.
:::

## The order to read it in

Read **Chapter 2** first, then **Chapter 17**. Those two attack your own
resume and your own projects, and they are the questions you are most likely
to be asked and least likely to have rehearsed.

Everything else you can read in any order. The chapter each section came from
in your original bank is marked, so you can cross-reference the two.

:::warning One thing before anything else
Fix the two stale README files, and check that your CI pipeline triggers on
the branch you actually use. That is an hour of work and it is worth more
than any answer in this book, because an interviewer who reads a stale README
concludes your resume is inflated when it is actually accurate.
:::
